# Four-minute demo video draft — working title

Status: **draft only**. Record only after owner acceptance, final naming, rule
eligibility confirmation, and a fresh capture of the exact submitted build.

Target duration: **3:35–3:50**. The narration may use Robert's explicitly approved
voice only after the separate voice and consent pipeline is verified. Until then,
use this as a shot list—not as evidence that narration exists.

## 0:00–0:20 — The problem

**Picture:** Custom desktop icon, then the native app window opening independently
of a browser. The companion idles, notices the visitor, and waves.

**Narration:**

> A lot of people need a calm place to talk, but they do not want every difficult
> moment to become a generic warning screen. This local-first companion keeps the
> conversation going, remembers what matters, and lets the person stay in control.

## 0:20–0:55 — A companion, not a form

**Picture:** Create a fictional profile called Elena. Show the procedural 3D
companion moving naturally, then enter: `I love Star Trek, sandwiches, and drawing.`

**Narration:**

> The companion is an animated three-dimensional character, not a slideshow. It
> supports everyday conversation and learns useful, device-local details over time.
> Memories remain reviewable and correctable instead of becoming hidden guesses.

## 0:55–1:30 — Grounded memory

**Picture:** Ask `What do I like?`; show the grounded answer and memory receipt.
Then correct one fictional detail and show the correction. Open the People/Memory
drawer and delete one fictional person.

**Narration:**

> Here it recalls only what this fictional profile actually shared. A person can
> correct a date, remove one relationship, forget a topic, or erase the profile.
> Optional password and guardian roles protect shared-home use, while someone who
> lives alone can keep that lock disabled.

## 1:30–2:05 — Context-aware support

**Picture:** Enter `I am angry with Sam. I feel alone tonight.` Show that the reply
does not suggest Sam. Then enter a low-mood prompt and show a gentle suggestion tied
to Elena's saved interests.

**Narration:**

> Relationship context changes the answer. If Sam is the source of the conflict,
> the companion does not blindly recommend calling Sam. It can instead suggest a
> familiar activity, a grounding exercise, a game, or simply keep talking.

## 2:05–2:40 — Difficult conversation without abandonment

**Picture:** Use the exact fictional test phrase `I feel like I do not want to be
alive, but please stay and talk with me.` Show the continued urgent conversation,
the immediate-danger question, and the `not in immediate danger` continuation.
Keep any optional emergency-resource text visible but secondary.

**Narration:**

> Difficult language routes to a deterministic safety conversation. The app asks
> what is happening now and stays present. It can keep optional emergency resources
> visible without replacing the conversation. This is software behavior—not a
> diagnosis, treatment, or promise of a clinical outcome.

## 2:40–3:10 — Offline and permission-respecting

**Picture:** Show the offline/local status receipt. Start hands-free mode, decline
the microphone permission, and immediately complete the same turn by typing. Show
that camera and screen capture remain unavailable.

**Narration:**

> The deterministic conversation, memories, reminders, games, and safety routing
> work locally. Microphone access is session-only and explicit; declining it never
> blocks text. Camera, screen capture, downloads, and external renderer requests are
> denied by the desktop shell.

## 3:10–3:35 — Useful every day

**Picture:** Show a corrected birthday reminder, a fictional doctor appointment,
one medication reminder that repeats only as configured, a short social-introduction
rehearsal, and the companion's breathing or happy-hop motion.

**Narration:**

> The same memory system supports corrected birthdays, appointments, prescribed-
> medication reminders, small-talk practice, calming activities, and simple games.
> The person can see why a response was chosen and remove what should not be kept.

## 3:35–3:48 — Close

**Picture:** Return to the companion, then the final Kira Labs/project card with the
public repository and demo links only after those links exist.

**Narration:**

> This working title is a private, local-first synthetic companion designed to keep
> listening, remember responsibly, and give people more control over support. It is
> ready for owner testing now; public release and clinical evaluation are later,
> separate gates.

## Capture rules

- Use only fictional names, contacts, medication, appointments, and life details.
- Capture the exact checksum-sealed submitted build, not the development server.
- Do not claim offline speech recognition or audible local voice until the exact
  packaged provider and playback path are proven.
- Do not claim clinical validation, diagnosis, treatment, guaranteed safety, emotion
  reading, or replacement of professional care.
- Blur local filesystem paths, account names, and raw receipts. Use only sanitized
  packaged-smoke evidence in the edit.
- Produce separate timed English captions. Translate captions only after the English
  timings and meaning are locked and reviewed.
